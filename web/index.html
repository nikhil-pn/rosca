The savings circle no one can run away with.

For generations, friends, families and office teams in India have saved together in chit funds. Everyone pays in each month and one member takes the pot. The weak spot has always been the organiser, who holds the money.

In Rosca the pot sits in a contract with no owner. It moves only when a majority of members approves and the winner signs with a post-quantum ML-DSA-87 key, checked on chain.

How a round works

Five steps, every round, like a chit fund you already know. The highlighted step is where the live circle is right now.

  1. 1

    Pay

    Every member pays the monthly share into the circle. Only the contract holds it.

  2. 2

    Bid

    Members who need money now bid the discount they will accept. The highest discount wins, as in a real chit auction, capped at 30%.

  3. 3

    Close

    When bidding ends, anyone can close the auction. With no bids, the earliest member who has not won yet takes the pot.

  4. 4

    Approve

    A majority of members approve the payout.

  5. 5

    Release

    The winner signs the payout with their ML-DSA-87 key. The contract checks it on chain with pqcVerify, pays the winner and shares the discount out to every member.

Winners keep paying: part of their pot is held back against their future shares. If a round stalls, members can dissolve the circle and take back what they paid in.

    Who can move the pot?

    Only the winner and a majority of members, together. Nobody can do it alone.

    ✕

    The organiser

    Opens the circle and nothing else. The contract has no owner, no admin, no upgrade and no withdraw function.

    ✕

    A thief with a member's wallet key

    A stolen secp256k1 key cannot make an ML-DSA-87 signature. Each member's EVM window is also scoped to this circle and fUSD, and only their post-quantum key can open a wider one.

    ✕

    A future quantum computer

    It could recover an ECDSA key from its public key. It cannot forge ML-DSA-87 (NIST FIPS 204, security level 5).

    ✓

    The winner, with a majority

    The payout needs most members' approvals and the winner's ML-DSA-87 signature over this circle, this round, the recipient, the amount and a deadline.

    The thief test, run for real

    Once per circle the keeper plays a thief who has stolen the winner's classical key. The thief sends release to their own address, either with a signature from their own ML-DSA-87 key or with the winner's real signature replayed for another recipient. Every attempt lands on chain and fails.

    • The first attempt runs in round 2 of the live circle.
